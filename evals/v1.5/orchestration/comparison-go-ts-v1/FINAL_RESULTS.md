# Go/TypeScript comparison: completed run and evidence limits

All **56 predeclared episodes** reached terminal markers once: 24 hard office
analysis cells, 24 Go-backend/TypeScript-UI full-stack cells, and eight separate
EN/KO continuation cells. No subject was restarted, coached or rerun; no
candidate was repaired outside the frozen same-tuple native flow. A terminal
episode is a recorded attempt, not necessarily an accepted artifact. The
original source, guide, client, six model/effort tuples, A/B/C/D dispatch and
each lane's one-shot invocation are fixed in [LIVE_COHORT_HANDOFF.md](LIVE_COHORT_HANDOFF.md)
and the committed O24/S24/continuity8 freezes. All three used the pinned
macOS Codex CLI 0.158.0-alpha.2 executable SHA-256
`c3e30211bd454da70ceb4d9cbc2e05fe6466812ab05c311c3bbff6addeb14202`.

`52994553d51e8fd65285165d39ea004be819e56da8dab99c22968591a165dc76`).

## What the comparison supports

The data show **no established overall quality or efficiency advantage** for
D on these two hard tasks. D produced two bounded office analysis passes and
one native-complete full-stack artifact that failed three external API
obligations; A produced the one externally passing S implementation scope but
not a formally passing design scope. Those are different evidence levels, not
a head-to-head win. Many subjects were blocked by native contracts or had
missing/unmapped evidence, and some frozen evaluator rules were narrower than
the public task. The single-task-per-tuple design, concurrent host load,
one failed process with unknown usage and lack of comparative eligible
performance measurements prevent a broad benefit or cost ranking. Any
follow-up **model comparison** must freeze a new version before its outcome
calls; these originals remain unchanged. Ordinary product fixes can use
appropriate focused tests.
