## Purpose

Use this procedure to reduce a market, product, or business-model hypothesis through a minimal learning experiment. It turns the riskiest assumption into a safe test with an actionable metric and a predeclared decision. An MVP here is a learning instrument, not an unfinished product, full launch plan, or proof of demand because something shipped.

## Use when

Use it when an important value, growth, adoption, or revenue hypothesis is unverified and a small observation could change whether to pivot, persevere, or stop. It fits a product or business decision with an identifiable target, bounded exposure, and an observable metric. Use it before expensive build-out when a reversible test can answer the question.

## Do not use when

Do not use a live minimal test when safety, law, privacy, security, or an irreversible customer or operational exposure makes the test inappropriate. Do not call a measurement-free release an experiment, or use a vanity metric as proof of learning. Do not use this method for a purely mechanical task, for a binding decision with no experimental discretion, or when the real problem is an unknown operational cause that should first be diagnosed.

## Inputs to establish

Establish the decision, target user or context, one falsifiable hypothesis, its riskiest assumption, and available evidence. Define the minimum test or MVP, exposure boundary, actionable metric and unit, observation window, threshold or qualitative rule, owner, and rollback or stop boundary. Record selection effects, access limits, and any baseline source. Ask no more than two concise questions, only when the answer could change test safety, the metric, or the next decision; otherwise mark the gap unverified and hold if decisive.

## Procedure

1. **Publish the hypothesis.** Write one conditional statement connecting an action or offer to a target context and observable outcome. Mark each ground as verified, computed, inferred, assumed, or unverified. **Visible output/state change:** falsifiable hypothesis.
2. **Publish the riskiest assumption.** Select the assumption whose failure would most change the decision and whose evidence is weakest or conflicted. State why it matters and what evidence would weaken it; preserve uncertainty rather than assigning a made-up likelihood. **Visible output/state change:** riskiest assumption.
3. **Publish the minimum test card.** Design the smallest reversible test with a limited audience or scope, artifact, duration, owner, consent or access boundary, and stop or rollback condition. If a live test creates material exposure, hold it and name a safer non-live check. **Visible output/state change:** minimum test card or hold.
4. **Publish the measurement specification.** Name the actionable metric, unit, source, observation window, baseline if available, and threshold or decision rule. Explain why the metric represents the learning question and label any working threshold as an assumption. **Visible output/state change:** checkable measurement state.
5. **Publish the result record.** After an authorized test, record expected versus observed behavior, missing observations, selection effects, deviations, and evidence state. Do not fill an absent result with a success story; no result leaves the hypothesis unverified. **Visible output/state change:** result record.
6. **Publish the next decision.** Apply the predeclared rule to choose pivot, persevere, stop, or hold. State the changed hypothesis or next test, owner, boundary, and one or two observable flip conditions. **Visible output/state change:** bounded decision.

## Public output contract

Return card-ready fields named `hypothesis`, `riskiest_assumption`, `minimum_test`, `metric_threshold`, and `next_decision`. The test must state its safe scope, observable signal, and stop or rollback condition. The metric must identify its source and unit, and the decision must distinguish pivot, persevere, stop, and hold. Include no more than two material uncertainties in the collapsed result. A missing decisive metric, threshold, or result produces an unverified or held outcome rather than an unqualified recommendation.

## Evidence and uncertainty rules

A shipped artifact is not evidence of demand, and a metric is not automatically evidence of causation. Label direct observations and linked sources as verified only when they support the specific ground; show inputs and expressions for computed results; mark model-based reasoning as inferred; mark a chosen threshold or untested premise as assumed; identify a missing check as unverified; and expose disagreements as conflicted. State the tested context, access or selection limits, and observation window. Do not invent probabilities, conversion rates, sample sizes, or customer reactions. One minimal test updates the tested hypothesis in that context; it does not generalize to every user or future period.

## Stop conditions

Hold when the decision, target context, riskiest assumption, actionable metric, or decision rule is missing and could change the outcome. Stop a test whose exposure is unsafe, unlawful, privacy-invasive, security-sensitive, irreversible, or outside authorization. Stop when no observable signal can distinguish the alternatives, when a required rollback is unavailable, or when the result is being inferred from a vanity metric. If the question is an operational failure with an unknown root condition, hand off rather than using a product experiment to mask it.

## Complement handoff

Hand off to `jobs-to-be-done` when the test cannot clarify the user’s underlying job, context, or desired progress and discovery must precede a product hypothesis. Pass the published target context, hypothesis, riskiest assumption, observed evidence state, and the unresolved user need only. Do not repeat the experiment or imply that a handoff supplies missing results. Use `assumption-mapping` first when several assumptions need prioritization before one can be tested.
