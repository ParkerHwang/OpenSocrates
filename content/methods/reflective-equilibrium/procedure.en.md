## Purpose

Use this procedure when considered case judgments and general principles pull in different directions and neither should be adjusted automatically. It makes the initial judgments and principles explicit, tests them against hard or counterexample cases, revises the least defensible element, and checks whether the resulting set is more coherent. The output is a transparent provisional position with remaining tension, not a claim that coherence alone proves moral or political truth.

## Use when

Use it for ethical policy, institutional design, professional practice, rights-sensitive decisions, or disputes where a principle seems sound but its application to a hard case feels wrong. It is appropriate when several judgments are considered rather than impulsive, the decision has discretion, and the background assumptions can be discussed. Use a named rule or authority first when one binding norm actually decides the matter.

## Do not use when

Do not use it to override a binding legal, safety, consent, or governance rule, or to manufacture discretion where none exists. Do not cherry-pick cases that support a preferred principle, and do not treat a coherent set as universally justified. Do not expose private personal information in case descriptions. If the relevant judgments are unconsidered, the principles are unknown, or revision would create immediate harm, hold the conclusion and gather safer inputs.

## Inputs to establish

List considered case judgments, candidate principles, background theory or values, affected people, decision scope, relevant authorities, and unresolved conflicts. Mark which judgments are direct, observed, reported, inferred, or assumed; remove details that are not needed to test the principle. Ask at most two decisive questions, such as “Which case would you refuse to sacrifice for the principle?” or “Is there a binding rule that leaves no discretion here?”

## Procedure

1. **Collect considered judgments.** State the initial judgments and the cases that make them stable rather than impulsive; include at least one difficult case that could challenge the set. **Visible output/state change:** publish a case-and-judgment register with evidence and privacy boundaries.
2. **State candidate principles.** Generalize the judgments into principles, conditions, exceptions, and background values without pretending that the generalization is already justified. **Visible output/state change:** publish the principle set and the judgment each principle is meant to explain.
3. **Test hard cases and conflicts.** Apply every principle to the difficult cases, counterexamples, affected groups, and plausible alternative framing. **Visible output/state change:** publish fits, conflicts, exceptions, and any principle that creates an unacceptable consequence.
4. **Choose a revision target.** Consider revising a case judgment, principle scope, exception, or background assumption; explain why the selected revision is less costly than the alternatives. **Visible output/state change:** publish a revision proposal and the trade-off it accepts.
5. **Recheck coherence and authority.** Run the revised set back through the cases and confirm that no binding rule has been displaced. Keep unresolved disagreement visible rather than averaging it away. **Visible output/state change:** publish the revised judgment-principle map and remaining tension.
6. **Set the provisional position.** State the current principle, case outcome, confidence in the fit, and what new case, evidence, or authority would require revision. **Visible output/state change:** publish a supported, provisional, or held conclusion with a flip condition and review owner.

## Public output contract

Return initial considered judgments, candidate principles, hard cases, conflicts, revision choice, revised fit, remaining tension, and current position. Separate case facts from value judgments and mark assumptions. The Conclusion Card should state the current position, its scope, unresolved tension, and an observable or authoritative condition that would change it; the trace can show the revision sequence without claiming private reasoning.

## Evidence and uncertainty rules

Coherence is not the same as truth, legality, or safety. A case judgment should be grounded in the stated facts and values, while a principle should be tested beyond the case that inspired it. Do not invent consensus, authority, or certainty. If a case is incomplete or a background value is assumed, mark it accordingly. If principles conflict and no justified priority is available, label the conclusion conflicted or held. Protect identities and omit unnecessary sensitive details.

## Stop conditions

Stop when no considered judgment or principle can be stated, when a binding rule decides without discretion, when a hard case cannot be described safely, or when revision would create immediate harm. Hold the conclusion if the remaining tension could change the action and no responsible priority rule is available. Escalate high-stakes ethical, legal, clinical, or governance questions.

## Complement handoff

Hand off to steelman-reasoning when a competing principle or opposing case has been dismissed before its strongest fair form was tested. Pass the public principles, hard cases, tension, and evidence gap; the complement should strengthen the opposing case without adding a new factual premise.
