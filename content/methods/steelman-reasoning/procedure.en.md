## Purpose

Use this procedure when an opposing view may contain a decisive insight or is being dismissed too quickly. It reconstructs the strongest fair version that the available wording and context actually support, identifies the evidence that version needs, and then tests it without weakening legitimate criticism. The output is not forced agreement. It is a more accurate opposing case, the part worth accepting, and the response or conclusion change that survives fair treatment.

## Use when

Use it for policy disputes, design reviews, strategy disagreements, public arguments, or a decision where one side is described with sarcasm, a label, or a weak example. It fits cases where the other position has a coherent goal, evidence, or trade-off that deserves examination. It is also useful before criticizing a proposal or rejecting a stakeholder concern. Work from the actual public claim and its context, not from a stereotype of the group that holds it.

## Do not use when

Do not invent a stronger position and attribute it to a person who did not express it. Do not steelman harmful instructions, abuse, dehumanization, or operational wrongdoing in a way that makes them more persuasive; summarize the safe issue and evaluate the risk instead. Do not use it when there is no genuine opposing case or when a binding rule decides the matter without discretion. A simple factual correction does not need a full opposing construction.

## Inputs to establish

Record the actual opposing claim, speaker’s stated goal, relevant context, audience, and decision affected. Separate what was said from a charitable interpretation, and mark the evidence offered, evidence missing, values in conflict, and constraints that cannot be negotiated. Ask at most two decisive questions, such as “What outcome is this view trying to protect?” or “Which version of the objection would change the decision if supported?”

## Procedure

1. **Anchor the actual position.** Quote or accurately paraphrase the public claim and distinguish it from labels, motives, or implications added by critics. **Visible output/state change:** publish the source claim, context, and scope; unexpressed extensions are marked as interpretations.
2. **State the strongest fair version.** Complete missing premises only when context makes them reasonable, preserve the speaker’s goal, and remove rhetorical weakness without changing the position’s direction. **Visible output/state change:** publish a charitable steelman and the assumptions added to make it coherent.
3. **Attach the needed support.** List the facts, values, trade-offs, or predictions the stronger version requires, and separate direct support from support that would still need checking. **Visible output/state change:** publish an evidence-and-assumption register with states such as verified, assumed, unverified, or conflicted.
4. **Find agreement and tension.** Identify which part of the steelman is reasonable or shared, then identify the strongest unresolved objection, affected party, or counterexample. **Visible output/state change:** publish accepted elements, remaining disputes, and any safety boundary.
5. **Test the strengthened case.** Compare it with the current conclusion under the same criteria and ask whether it changes the recommendation, mitigation, or wording. **Visible output/state change:** publish the response to the steelman and a recorded conclusion change, no-change reason, or unresolved conflict.
6. **Set the fair verdict.** State whether the opposing case is supported, conditionally supported, weakened, or still untested, and what evidence would change that status. **Visible output/state change:** publish the updated conclusion and one observable flip condition without claiming that charitable reconstruction proves truth.

## Public output contract

Return the actual opposing claim, strongest fair reconstruction, support it needs, points of agreement, strongest remaining objection, and response or conclusion change. Clearly label added assumptions and distinguish fair interpretation from a direct quotation. A Conclusion Card may show the material alternative and its effect on the current judgment; the trace can retain the claim lineage. Do not expose private reasoning or use the method to launder a harmful position.

## Evidence and uncertainty rules

The strongest version must remain faithful to the speaker’s purpose and public wording. Charity is not evidence, and a coherent argument is not a verified argument. Do not invent sources, motives, expertise, or probabilities. If the position depends on a value choice, label it as such; if it depends on an unverified factual premise, mark the result provisional. If a credible opposing case remains unresolved, lower or hold the conclusion rather than calling the debate settled.

## Stop conditions

Stop when the actual position cannot be identified, when reconstruction would add a material claim the speaker did not make, or when the topic is harmful and a stronger restatement would increase risk. Hold the verdict when the steelman depends on missing decisive evidence. If the issue is governed by a binding rule, report the rule and permitted discretion instead of manufacturing a balance.

## Complement handoff

Hand off to reflective-equilibrium when the strengthened objection exposes a conflict between principles and hard cases that needs mutual revision. Pass the public steelman, evidence gaps, values in tension, and changed judgment; the complement should not restate the opposing case as a new fact.
