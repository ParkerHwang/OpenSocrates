## Purpose

Use this procedure to make the people and organizations that can affect or be affected by a decision visible. It connects each material stakeholder’s interests, power, impact, consent, conflict, and implementation role to an engagement owner and next action. The aim is not to produce a long name list or a decorative grid; it is to show whose response can change the decision or whether the decision can be implemented.

## Use when

Use it when implementation depends on cooperation, consent, adoption, resistance, oversight, or informal influence. It is useful when decision rights or veto points are unclear, when affected people are outside the formal team, or when a communication and participation plan must be grounded in interests rather than volume. Include people who are affected but not represented in the current decision process.

## Do not use when

Do not use it when stakeholders are already aligned, materially unaffected, and the remaining task is only to compare options or verify evidence. Do not use it for a pure macro-environment scan, a local process diagnosis, or a mechanical request to format a contact list. Do not equate formal authority with legitimacy, interest with power, or a loud voice with material impact. Do not infer opposition or support without a basis.

## Inputs to establish

Establish the decision question, intended outcome, decision owner, implementation boundary, time horizon, affected groups, formal authority, constraints, and evidence available about interests or likely effects. Separate involved actors from affected-but-not-involved groups. Ask at most two concise questions, and only when the decision owner, affected population, or authority path could change the engagement recommendation. If an actor is only suspected, mark the entry as a hypothesis.

## Procedure

1. Publish the decision, intended outcome, implementation boundary, and accountable owner. Mark the boundary as user-provided or assumed; this creates the visible participation state. Visible output/state change: Participation state published.
2. Publish an actor register that includes decision makers, implementers, blockers, beneficiaries, bearers of cost, oversight bodies, and affected-but-not-involved groups. State each actor’s stake and the evidence or reason for inclusion. Visible output/state change: Actor register published.
3. Publish power, interest, impact, and consent posture for each material actor. Distinguish formal authority from informal influence and measured effect from inferred concern; use ordinal comparisons only when their basis is visible. Visible output/state change: Power-impact map published.
4. Publish the material alignments, conflicts, dependencies, and missing voices. For each conflict, identify the issue, parties, evidence state, and whether the disagreement can change the decision or only the implementation route. Visible output/state change: Conflict register published.
5. Publish an engagement and ownership plan. For each material actor, state the owner, contact purpose, participation or information needed, decision right, timing, and observable response that would change the plan. Visible output/state change: Engagement plan published.
6. Publish an omission and consent check for people affected outside the formal map. Add a group only when the decision or evidence supports material relevance, and record an objection or appeal route when consent or harm is decisive; this updates the actor map. Visible output/state change: Actor map updated.
7. Publish the current recommendation, unresolved conflict, and flip condition. If a decisive actor, impact, or consent fact is missing, publish a held or provisional result and the smallest check needed rather than declaring implementation ready. Visible output/state change: Hold state published.

## Public output contract

Return a compact result with `decision`, `stakeholders`, `stakes`, `power_impact`, `consent`, `conflicts`, `engagement_owner`, `observable_response`, and `flip_condition`. Include affected-but-unrepresented groups when material. Grounds use **verified**, **computed**, **inferred**, **assumed**, **unverified**, or **conflicted**; an actor’s presence is not evidence of an effect. The conclusion states whether the decision is supported, conditionally supported, or held pending participation or evidence.

## Evidence and uncertainty rules

Use a source, user-provided record, decision right, observed behavior, reproducible calculation, or clearly marked inference for each material stake, power, impact, or consent claim. Keep formal authority, resources, legitimacy, interest, and influence as distinct attributes. Do not assign numerical influence or confidence without a defensible scale and provenance. Do not treat a majority view or decision owner’s preference as proof that an omitted group is immaterial. Mark disputed interests, predicted reactions, and uncontacted groups as inferred, unverified, or conflicted.

## Stop conditions

Stop and ask one blocking question when different decision owners or affected populations imply materially different engagement plans and no governing mandate selects one. Hold when a decisive actor, harm, consent requirement, or implementation dependency cannot be identified or checked. Stop when the stakeholder map is immaterial to the decision and hand off to the method that addresses the actual comparison or external scan. End when the map, conflict, owner, next engagement action, and flip condition are public.

## Complement handoff

Hand off to `game-theory` only as a focused complement when a stakeholder conflict depends on strategic responses, incentives, information, or credible commitments. Pass the actor register, stakes, power and impact evidence, conflicts, decision rights, and engagement constraints. Do not repeat stakeholder discovery or ask the complement to decide whose interests count.
