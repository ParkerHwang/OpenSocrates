## Purpose

Use this procedure to choose a response mode from the observed shape of a situation. It distinguishes a repeatable cause-and-effect pattern from a situation that needs expert analysis, safe experiments, urgent containment, or better framing. The purpose is not to decorate a recommendation with a domain label. It is to make the classification evidence, the action that follows, and the signal that could change the classification visible.

## Use when

Use it when the proposed response may be mismatched to the situation: for example, when a team is applying a fixed process to an adaptive problem, waiting for analysis during a fast-moving disruption, or treating several different problems as one. It is useful when the choice between standardizing, analyzing, probing, and acting is itself the decision. It can also separate a mixed situation into parts that require different responses.

## Do not use when

Do not use it for a purely mechanical task, a settled rule with no meaningful discretion, or a request that only needs a known calculation or transcription. Do not force a classification when the user wants a decorative label rather than a change in action. Do not treat a complex situation as automatically more important, or a clear situation as automatically safe; the classification describes the decision relationship, not the severity of consequences.

## Inputs to establish

Establish the decision question, the boundary of the situation, the time horizon, the observed pattern, and any immediate safety or continuity constraint. Collect observations, prior examples, domain expertise, and user-supplied facts, while marking what is checked, inferred, assumed, or still unverified. Ask at most two concise questions, and only when the answer could change the action mode. If the decisive observation is unavailable, state a provisional boundary and continue only to a clearly marked hold.

## Procedure

1. Publish a one-sentence decision question, scope, time horizon, and immediate constraint. Mark each element as user-provided or assumed; this creates the visible framing state. Visible output/state change: Framing state published.
2. Publish a causal-observation record that separates repeatable relationships, expert-dependent relationships, emerging feedback, acute disruption, and observations that cannot yet be interpreted. Attach an evidence state to each material observation. Visible output/state change: Observation record published.
3. Publish one provisional classification—clear, complicated, complex, chaotic, or confused—with the two or three observations that support it. If the evidence supports different classifications for different parts, split the situation and publish the separate boundaries instead of averaging them. Visible output/state change: Classification state published.
4. Publish the matching action mode: sense–categorize–respond for a repeatable pattern, sense–analyze–respond for an expert-dependent pattern, probe–sense–respond for an adaptive pattern, or act–sense–respond for an urgent disruption. For a confused classification, publish the smallest framing or observation task that can remove the confusion. Visible output/state change: Action mode published.
5. Publish one safe next action and its learning or transition signals. State what will be observed, by whom, and when; a signal must be observable rather than a general hope that the situation improves. Visible output/state change: Signal register published.
6. Publish the conclusion state, its evidence, one material uncertainty, and the condition that would flip the classification or action mode. Do not claim certainty merely because a label was selected. Visible output/state change: Conclusion state published.
7. If a missing boundary, observation, or constraint could change the action mode, publish a held or provisional result and the blocking question or check. This changes the state to hold rather than filling the gap with an invented fact. Visible output/state change: Hold state published.

## Public output contract

Return a compact, card-ready result containing `decision_question`, `domain`, `classification_evidence`, `action_mode`, `transition_signals`, `next_action`, and `flip_condition`. The domain is a conclusion supported by visible observations, not a badge. Grounds use **verified**, **computed**, **inferred**, **assumed**, **unverified**, or **conflicted** where applicable. Include no more than three material assumptions and two unresolved uncertainties. If the result is held, say what is missing and what check releases the hold.

## Evidence and uncertainty rules

Treat direct observations, identified sources, user-provided records, and reproducible calculations as evidence only for what they actually support. A label is normally an inference from the pattern; it is not verified just because a source uses the same word. Do not invent probabilities for transition, impact, or success. Separate the fact that feedback exists from an inference about what it means. Mark a boundary change, competing classification, or stale observation as conflicted or unverified, and lower the conclusion when that uncertainty could change the next action.

## Stop conditions

Stop and ask one blocking question when two plausible boundaries or observations require materially different actions and neither can be chosen from the user’s purpose or an authoritative constraint. Hold when the decisive relation, immediate constraint, or transition signal cannot be established. End the procedure when one actionable mode, one observable signal, and one flip condition are published. Do not wait for exhaustive analysis in an urgent situation; record the containment action and revisit the classification after new observations.

## Complement handoff

Hand off to `scenario-planning` only when the classification exposes several coherent external futures that require different preparations. Pass the published boundary, classification evidence, action mode, transition signals, and unresolved uncertainty. Do not repeat the full classification or ask the complement to supply a missing fact that should first release this procedure’s hold.
