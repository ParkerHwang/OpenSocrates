## Purpose

Use this procedure to identify material changes in the external political, economic, social, technological, environmental, and legal context and connect them to a strategic decision. It is a scoped evidence scan, not a requirement to fill six empty checklist boxes. The result should show which factors can change an option, over what horizon, through what mechanism, and what signal would require a reassessment.

## Use when

Use it when market, policy, industry, demographic, technological, environmental, or legal conditions may change the viability or timing of a strategy. It is useful before a market entry, product direction, investment, policy response, or external-risk review, and when an external section of a broader strategy needs evidence rather than generic trends. Limit the scan to factors that are material to the stated decision.

## Do not use when

Do not use it for a local operational failure whose cause is inside a process, for an internal capability review, or for a mechanical request to copy or summarize known facts. Do not treat a broad list of headlines as a PESTEL conclusion. Do not imply that all six dimensions are equally important, that a factor is causal merely because it is adjacent to the decision, or that an unverified current law is settled advice.

## Inputs to establish

Establish the decision question, organization or product, geography and jurisdiction, sector, baseline, time horizon, material dependencies, and the options that could be affected. Identify the source date and status for any current policy, market, demographic, technology, environmental, or legal claim. Ask at most two concise questions, and only when geography, horizon, or decision scope could change the recommendation. If a scope is missing but a safe provisional scope is possible, state it and mark the result assumed.

## Procedure

1. Publish the decision question, geographic and sector boundary, baseline, and time horizon. Mark what is user-provided versus assumed; this creates the visible analysis scope. Visible output/state change: Scope state published.
2. Publish a six-factor register containing only material political, economic, social, technological, environmental, and legal factors. For each factor, include its source or provenance, date, evidence state, and the option or dependency it touches. Visible output/state change: Factor register published.
3. Publish the mechanism and direction for each retained factor: explain how the change could affect demand, cost, access, compliance, capability, timing, or legitimacy. Replace generic “positive” or “negative” labels with a checkable causal link. Visible output/state change: Mechanism links published.
4. Publish an ordinal view of direction, speed, exposure, and uncertainty with a short basis for each judgment. Use labels such as low, medium, or high only as transparent comparisons; do not turn them into unsupported percentages or forecasts. Visible output/state change: Ordinal assessment published.
5. Publish the opportunity, threat, or monitoring status for each material factor and rank the few factors that can change the decision. State whether the status is verified, inferred, assumed, unverified, or conflicted. Visible output/state change: Materiality state published.
6. Publish one to three strategic implications, including a no-regret action, a contingent action, or a targeted research check when appropriate. Where factors interact, state the interaction and its evidence rather than treating correlation as proof. Visible output/state change: Implication set published.
7. Publish the current judgment, decisive grounds, remaining uncertainty, and observable signals that would flip the implication. If a missing factor, source, jurisdiction, or horizon is decisive, publish a held result and the smallest check needed before choosing. Visible output/state change: Judgment state published.

## Public output contract

Return a compact, card-ready result with `scope`, `horizon`, `material_factors`, `evidence_status`, `mechanisms`, `opportunity_threat_monitoring`, `strategic_implications`, `signals`, and `flip_condition`. Include only factors that can change the stated decision; group related items rather than listing every trend. Grounds use **verified**, **computed**, **inferred**, **assumed**, **unverified**, or **conflicted**. State which implication is current and which alternatives or checks remain live.

## Evidence and uncertainty rules

Use current, identifiable sources for checkable claims and preserve the source date and jurisdiction. A source that describes a trend does not by itself establish its magnitude, local effect, or strategic consequence. Separate observation, interpretation, and recommendation. Do not claim an exhaustive scan, invent probabilities, or convert ordinal exposure into a numerical forecast without a defensible model and provenance. If credible sources disagree, show the conflict and lower or hold the affected implication. Unverified legal or regulatory information must not support an unqualified compliance conclusion.

## Stop conditions

Stop and ask one blocking question when two plausible geographies, jurisdictions, or horizons lead to materially different implications and the user’s purpose cannot choose between them. Hold when a current rule, external dependency, or factor is decisive but cannot be checked. Stop the procedure when the task is only a local operational diagnosis or internal comparison; route that question to a more suitable method. End when material factors, mechanisms, implications, signals, and a flip condition are public.

## Complement handoff

Hand off to `scenario-planning` when the material external uncertainties form several coherent futures that require different preparations. Pass the scoped factor register, evidence states, interactions, implications, and signposts. Do not ask the complement to repeat the scan or fill an unsupported PESTEL category; it should use the published uncertainties to construct futures and contingent actions.
