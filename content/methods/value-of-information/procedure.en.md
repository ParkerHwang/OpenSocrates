## Purpose

Use this procedure to decide whether obtaining specific information is worth its cost and delay before acting. It asks whether the information can change the available action at a decision threshold, not whether the topic is interesting or whether more research would be reassuring. The output is a bounded choice among investigate, act now, monitor, or stop searching; it may be held when the action threshold or information path is missing.

## Use when

Use it when a person or team is choosing between acting now and testing, researching, monitoring, inspecting, or waiting. It fits when one or more uncertain variables could change the preferred action, when information has a feasible collection method, and when the cost of delay or exposure can be described. It is useful for deciding which question to answer first, whether a pilot is decision-relevant, or when a threshold should end an investigation.

## Do not use when

Do not use it for open-ended curiosity, a request for a general literature summary with no decision attached, or a case where no new information could change the action. Do not use it when a binding rule fixes the action regardless of evidence. Do not invent a probability that research will succeed, a monetary value for an unpriced outcome, or an expected benefit merely because the information sounds useful. If the decision is mechanical, use the mechanical calculation or verification path instead.

## Inputs to establish

State the decision, current options, action threshold, uncertain variable, current evidence, possible information source, collection method, direct cost, delay, exposure while waiting, and what action each possible finding would trigger. Record provenance and evidence state for the threshold and uncertainty. Use a qualitative value comparison when probabilities or money are not defensible. Ask at most two decisive questions, such as “What observable finding would change the action?” and “What is the safe limit on cost or delay?”

## Procedure

1. **Frame the action.** Define the decision, available actions, current preferred action, time boundary, and the threshold or condition that would change it. **Visible output/state change:** publish an action-threshold frame and mark an absent threshold as unverified.
2. **Name the uncertainty.** Identify the smallest uncertain variable that could change the action, its current evidence, plausible findings, and the source or test that could distinguish them. Do not bundle several interesting questions into one research request. **Visible output/state change:** publish an uncertainty register with evidence states and a finding-to-action map.
3. **Check action-changing value.** For each possible finding, state which action would follow and whether that action is materially different from acting now. If every credible finding leaves the action unchanged, stop searching. **Visible output/state change:** publish an action-change test marked route-to-information, act-now, or no decision value.
4. **Compare collection burden.** Describe direct cost, delay, opportunity cost, safety exposure, feasibility, and reversibility. Compare them with the consequence of acting on the wrong side of the threshold; do not convert non-comparable harms into unsupported currency. **Visible output/state change:** publish an information-cost and delay register with supported, assumed, and unresolved entries.
5. **Set a bounded recommendation.** Choose investigate, act now, monitor, or stop, and define a stopping rule based on an observable result, time limit, or threshold. Use ranges or qualitative labels only when their provenance is stated; do not fabricate expected probabilities or value. **Visible output/state change:** publish the recommendation, collection scope, stop rule, and residual uncertainty.
6. **Hold when decisive input is absent.** If the threshold, action alternatives, feasible information path, or cost/delay limit is missing and could change the choice, hold the recommendation and request only the smallest safe clarification. **Visible output/state change:** publish the decision threshold, uncertain variable, possible information, cost and delay, recommendation, and a Conclusion Card flip condition.

## Public output contract

Return the current decision and action alternatives, decision threshold, uncertain variable, possible information and its provenance, action-changing finding map, collection cost, delay and exposure, feasibility, stopping rule, and recommendation. State whether information could change the action. A recommendation to investigate must say what will be checked and when the search stops; a recommendation to act now must say why the remaining uncertainty is not decision-changing. Keep calculations and source dates in the trace.

## Evidence and uncertainty rules

Mark a threshold or observed fact verified only when its source directly supports it. Mark transparent arithmetic computed, consequences inferred, planning premises assumed, unchecked claims unverified, and unresolved disagreement conflicted. Do not use research quality as a substitute for action relevance. Do not treat a possible answer as valuable unless it maps to a different action, and do not assert expected value, success probability, sample adequacy, or monetary benefit without defensible inputs. If uncertainty and source quality disagree, show both and lower the recommendation.

## Stop conditions

Hold when no action threshold, alternative action, uncertain variable, feasible information method, or cost/delay boundary can be stated. Stop when all credible findings leave the action unchanged, when waiting creates unacceptable exposure, or when a binding rule decides independently of information. If a high-stakes decision needs missing evidence, state the smallest safe check and seek qualified review.

## Complement handoff

Hand off to bayesian-updating when the uncertain variable and evidence stream are defined and new observations can update a stated belief or range. Pass the public threshold, prior evidence state, observation plan, decision-change map, and stopping rule only; do not pass invented probabilities or hidden reasoning.
