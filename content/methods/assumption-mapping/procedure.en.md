## Purpose

Use this procedure to make the beliefs supporting an idea visible, separate importance from evidence, and select the smallest safe test for assumptions that could change the decision. It produces a prioritized learning surface, not a truth verdict.

## Use when

Use it before committing to a product idea, operating change, research direction, or judgment that depends on uncertain beliefs. It is useful when a team cannot agree on what must be true, when evidence is uneven, or when possible tests need an explicit order. It is especially helpful before a reversible experiment or resource commitment while the decision can still change safely.

## Do not use when

Do not use it for a mechanical request, a binding rule with no discretion, or an irreversible decision. Do not expose private or sensitive assumptions merely to complete a map; restrict the result to authorized information. Do not invent a test when no observable outcome can distinguish the assumption, or when testing would create legal, safety, privacy, or material operational exposure. If the work is a market experiment, hand off the selected assumption to `lean-startup` rather than turning this map into a full report.

## Inputs to establish

Establish the decision question, desired outcome, proposed action, and what would materially change the decision. Gather candidate assumptions as testable statements and classify each as desirability, viability, feasibility, or another named domain. Record the source, user observation, calculation, or inference for each. Define the smallest safe test options, constraints, owner, observation window, and decision rule. Ask no more than two concise questions, only when the missing answer could change the riskiest set or test safety; otherwise mark it assumed.

## Procedure

1. **Publish the frame.** State the decision question, intended outcome, scope, and decision boundary in one compact record. Mark each boundary item as user-provided, verified from a source, inferred, or assumed. **Visible output/state change:** framing state.
2. **Publish the assumption register.** Rewrite each belief as a statement that could be supported, weakened, or left unresolved by an observation. Attach its domain and the consequence of being false. **Visible output/state change:** labelled assumption register.
3. **Publish the two axes.** Place each assumption on an ordinal importance axis and an evidence-state axis. Use labels such as high, medium, or low only with a stated basis, and use `verified`, `computed`, `inferred`, `assumed`, `unverified`, or `conflicted` for support. **Visible output/state change:** importance/evidence map.
4. **Publish the riskiest set.** Select assumptions whose failure would materially change the outcome and whose support is weakest or conflicted. Explain each selection, preserve indistinguishable ties, and record unresolved uncertainty. **Visible output/state change:** riskiest set and uncertainty.
5. **Publish a safe test card.** For each selected assumption, name the least costly reversible test, observable signal, observation window, owner, and decision rule. Include a stop or rollback boundary. **Visible output/state change:** safe test card or hold.
6. **Publish the decision map.** State what a supporting, weakening, conflicting, or missing signal would change: continue, revise, seek evidence, or hold. **Visible output/state change:** provisional conclusion and flip conditions.

## Public output contract

Return card-ready fields named `assumptions`, `importance_evidence_axes`, `riskiest_set`, `next_test`, and `flip_condition`. Each assumption must retain its domain, consequence, and evidence state. `next_test` must contain an observable signal, a safe boundary, and a decision rule; it must not promise that the test proves the whole idea. Include at most three material assumptions and two unresolved uncertainties in the collapsed result, with further branches left to the trace. If a decisive assumption or test boundary is missing, state that the result is held or provisional.

## Evidence and uncertainty rules

An assumption is not a fact because it is repeated, important, or plausible. Link `verified` claims to a supporting source when available; show inputs for `computed` claims; label reasoning from other grounds as `inferred`; adopted premises as `assumed`; the missing check for `unverified`; and disagreement for `conflicted`. Use ordinal importance only when the consequence or decision dependency is stated. Do not invent probabilities, sample sizes, or test results. A test signal updates the assumption only in the tested context; it does not generalize without further support.

## Stop conditions

Hold the result when the decision question, material consequence, or authorization to expose an assumption is missing. Hold when two assumptions remain tied and the missing evidence could change the test order. Stop before proposing a test if its exposure is unsafe, unlawful, irreversible, or not meaningfully observable. If the task is mechanical or the decision has no discretion, stop this method and route to the applicable direct or policy response. Do not convert an empty map into a recommendation.

## Complement handoff

Hand off to `lean-startup` when the published riskiest assumption is a market, product, value, growth, or revenue hypothesis that needs a minimal learning experiment. Pass only the assumption statement, domain, consequence, evidence state, selected safe signal, and decision rule. Do not repeat the full map or imply that the complement has already run the test. Use `value-of-information` separately when the open question is primarily whether additional research is worth its cost.
