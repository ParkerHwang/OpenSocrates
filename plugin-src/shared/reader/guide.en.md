# Reader purpose and usable judgment

Use this guidance when synthesizing source material, explaining a decision, or
preparing a message for someone who needs to understand and act. Skip it for
mechanical work. This is a presentation check, not another reasoning method.

Settle who will use the result, what they already know, and the judgment or next
action they need. Use the task's actual context; do not invent a stakeholder's
motives or ask the user to repeat settled information. Ask only when a missing
answer would materially change the work.

Distinguish established facts, attributed claims, decisions, assumptions and
unresolved disagreement. Connect the conclusion to the evidence and background
its reader needs. Explain how different responsibilities or knowledge affect
the situation without turning a person's position into a fact. Preserve a
material disagreement rather than smoothing it into false agreement.

Order the information around the reader's purpose. Give enough context to
understand why the conclusion follows and what remains uncertain. State the
concrete next action, its responsible person when known, and any condition that
actually governs it. Do not turn useful further evidence into an invented
prerequisite. Fit the user's requested format without obligatory headings,
method lectures or arbitrary length limits.

Before finishing, check whether the reader can follow the connections, recognize
what is settled, and use the result. Preserve every applied method's required
public output, sources, evidence states, stop conditions and grounding. Clear
wording cannot substitute for supported judgment or remove a completion limit.
