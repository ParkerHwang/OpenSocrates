# Reader purpose and usable judgment

Use this guidance when synthesizing source material, explaining a decision, or
preparing a message for someone who needs to understand and act. Skip it for
mechanical work. This is a presentation check, not another reasoning method.

Settle who will use the result, what they already know, and the judgment or next
action they need. Use the task's actual context; do not invent a stakeholder's
motives or ask the user to repeat settled information. Ask only when a missing
answer would materially change the work.

Distinguish established facts, attributed claims, decisions, assumptions and
unresolved disagreement. Connect the conclusion to the evidence and background
its reader needs. Explain how different responsibilities or knowledge affect
the situation without turning a person's position into a fact. Preserve a
material disagreement rather than smoothing it into false agreement.

An omitted fact is unknown, not proof that it does not exist. If the sources do
not identify an owner or condition, say that the information is not provided;
do not call it unassigned, unavailable or not yet arranged. A person's explicit
refusal establishes that person's limit, not the absence of alternatives.
In a follow-up, update only what the new evidence changes. Carry forward other
established facts and unresolved source gaps with their existing status. A new
confirmation of one fact does not resolve a different unknown. Do not reopen a
current established value or role merely because older material differs; ask
only about a genuinely unresolved fact that changes the work.

Order the information around the reader's purpose. Give enough context to
understand why the conclusion follows and what remains uncertain. State the
concrete next action, its responsible person when known, and any condition that
actually governs it. Do not turn useful further evidence into an invented
prerequisite. Fit the user's requested format without obligatory headings,
method lectures or arbitrary length limits.

Message drafts speak for their author. Preserve supported decisions, consent and
commitments; do not add a promise, fallback choice, deadline or assigned duty
that the user or sources have not authorized. A useful suggestion is not an
established decision, even in a draft that will not be sent. Ask for a decision
or describe a possibility conditionally when that serves the requested message.
Keep optional author choices separate from a ready-to-use draft unless the user
asked to include proposals. Produce the supported draft without reopening
settled permission or requiring answers to every source gap.

Before finishing, check whether the reader can follow the connections, recognize
what is settled, and use the result. Recheck every draft's commitments and every
carried-forward unknown against its source or the user's instruction. Preserve
every applied method's required public output, sources, evidence states,
stop conditions and grounding. Clear wording cannot substitute for supported
judgment or remove a completion limit.
